Handoff to the deep dive: Ottawa's forestry money is promised, the Quesnel mill is already idled over fibre supply, and the question left hanging is who sets the rules for projects on the land in between.

Deep-dive question: With Ottawa promising $6.5 billion for forestry over six years from 2027/28 while fast-tracking project review through Bill C-39, does the Tŝilhqot'in Nation's new impact assessment framework give working Cariboo industries a clearer route for investment, or one more gate between a curtailed mill town and its restart?

Riley starts: A Nation-designed review process with published values and criteria gives capital and workers a known path, and a project that passes it is more likely to last than one rushed through.
Casey starts: Fast-track timelines and funding timelines are being set elsewhere, so the mill workers and towns who carry the risk wait on an unresolved clash of processes while the owners keep the margin.

  1. The Tŝilhqot'in framework turns the Nation's say over projects into a published process with stated criteria, rather than a reaction to each proposal.
    then, because: If the two processes may collide, the question is what a project proponent or a mill owner is actually waiting on, which brings in the money and its timing.
  2. The federal forestry money and the mill closures are on different clocks, so what working towns need now is certainty about rules, not only cash later.
    then, because: If the money is aimed at keeping existing operations going, the next question is who it reaches and who bears the risk if it doesn't work.
  3. Federal funding language favours existing operators and owners, so the risk stays with workers and towns while the investment decisions sit with companies.
    then, because: Both hosts need the case to land somewhere specific, so we turn to who in the Cariboo would actually be in that room.
  4. In the Cariboo, a Nation's review framework and a mill town's restart hopes meet in the same neighbourhood, and neither is currently guaranteed a place in the other's timeline.
    then, because: With the link narrowed, what remains is who answers to a mill town when two processes disagree, which is the landing.

Landing: Held open: when a Nation's own impact assessment and a federal fast-track both claim to review the same kind of project, whose decision should promised forestry capital wait on, and who is responsible for telling a mill town like Quesnel where things stand in the meantime.
