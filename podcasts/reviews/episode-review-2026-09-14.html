Episode Review — Cariboo Signals, September 14, 2026

Published September 14, 2026 · Cariboo Signals

What the pipeline did, what broke, and what it chose when something broke. Run 34820905946.

The body-backfill budget ran dry

The pipeline spent all 12 Claude calls available for expanding thin article summaries. Forty-three roundup stories were dropped from the queue rather than shipped with stub bodies. The decision was mechanical: when the backfill budget depleted, remaining articles below a density threshold stayed sparse and got cut.

This is the constraint made visible. The script reached 2,995 words—495 short of the 3,400 target—because there were fewer bodies to write from. The first draft came in at 2,565 words and was sent back for an expand pass, but the expand pass had fewer materials to work with than it needed.

Citation alignment held unevenly

Roundup citations matched 12 of 15 sources. Deep dives matched 2 of 3. The three deep-dive stories were vetted—Star Trek and technology, two Hackaday pieces on infinity mirrors and 35mm film—but one citation fell away in the rendering.

The anchor framing went unvariable

The debate question, "Who decides what stories get told and preserved in local communities, and how can digital tools democratize both the creation and archiving of cultural memory?", had no per-weekday framings. Each anchor segment frames the question unaided. This is noted as a degradation but not a failure—the question runs the same every day the episode airs.

Voice and pacing met spec

Casey spoke 91% as much as Riley. Two AI tell-pattern hits flagged in quality review. The 20-minute episode encoded to 18.3 MB audio and 63.4 MB video. Episode 230 shipped to RSS.

Three manual runs, one fallback 409 minutes late

The pipeline ran three times by hand between 8:05 AM and 10:05 AM Pacific. Script generation took 1,159 seconds; audio render took 456 seconds; the full run from checkout to YouTube upload completed in under 35 minutes each time. A fourth run, fallback trigger 2 at 3:05 AM, started at 4:54 PM—409 minutes behind schedule—and remained in progress at review time.
