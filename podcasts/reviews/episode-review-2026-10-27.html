What happened

The script shipped at 3955 words, well above the 3400-word target

The first draft came in at 3158 words against a 3400 target, so it was sent back for up to two expand passes. The script that shipped ran to 3955 words, 555 over target. Read the drafts with that in mind: the expansion added length rather than correcting a shortfall the listener would have heard.

Casey carried 52% of the deep-dive words and spoke at a 1.14 ratio over Riley. The pipeline does not balance hosts by word count, so this is the default behaviour showing through, not a fault. Check whether the expand prompt is steering toward whichever host is already ahead.

Roundup citations matched on 11 of 18 items, against 3 of 4 in the deep dives

Eleven of the 18 roundup citations matched their sources, and 3 of the 4 deep-dive citations matched. The roundup gap is the weaker point: seven roundup citations did not align with their stories. Forty roundup stories were dropped by the airtime budget (NEWS_ROUNDUP_COUNT), so the pool is larger than one segment can air and cut stories remain eligible for later days. Whether the seven misaligned citations come from the stories that aired or from the pool is not separated in the figures.

Tighten the citation check in the roundup stage so that a mismatch is caught before the script is written, not after.

Debate question asks who controls mining benefit and whether local standing gives a say

The debate question for the day was: who actually holds control and benefit from mining and processing ventures, and does local standing (crews, ranch families, Indigenous claims) translate into a real say in outcomes?
