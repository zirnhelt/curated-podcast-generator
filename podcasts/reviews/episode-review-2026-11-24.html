The plan

Roundup: 9/15 adjacent stories aired in the plan's order; 13/17 bridges name a shared thing (the rest are clean pivots). Deep dive: 6/6 planned beats aired.

Roundup frame: A news day where the Interior's mines, burned forests and cattle numbers sit beside farm stories from far afield, all about who works the land and who profits from it.

Named bridges:

  • Copper mining and the people who work it: from an exploration option near Mount Polley to a strike at Highland Valley Copper.
  • Logan Lake and the Highland Valley strike: a Tyee piece on the same mine town and its strike belongs here, though it airs later in the theme block.
  • Wildfire and forest land: leaving the mine towns, the next story is about land burned around Clinton.
  • Wildfire risk work in the South Cariboo: from the burned Clinton forest to pile burning near 100 Mile House.
  • Resource industries and who captures their value: from Interior politics to a Brazilian push to process minerals at home.
  • Minerals and who controls the route out: Brazil's processing push leads to Pakistan, where transport matters as much as geology.
  • Wood products and where the capacity goes: from minerals to a B.C. lumber company weighing a mill overseas.
  • Agriculture and ranching: moving from forestry to cattle supply in the United States.
  • Farm economics and input costs: from tight cattle supply to Swedish farmers facing fertilizer and energy price rises.
  • Energy inputs on the farm: Swedish fuel costs lead to Polish propane shortages hitting corn drying.
  • Climate pressure on farms: from a fuel shortage at harvest to drought, flood and heat strategies.
  • Small-scale farm income: from climate adaptation to small farms finding revenue in saffron, seed processing and direct sales.
  • Farming at the opposite scale: from small direct-sale farms to a huge desert dairy in Algeria.

Handoff to the deep dive: Who keeps the margin when the land and the work stay put: the mine crews, the burned community forest and the farms in these stories all show people holding on while costs and decisions come from elsewhere.

Deep-dive question: When a farm or ranch family keeps working the same land through thin-margin years, are the tools and contracts around them letting them keep the value of that staying, or is staying simply what's left when the margin has gone to retailers, input suppliers and investors?
