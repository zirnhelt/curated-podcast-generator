Episode Review — Cariboo Signals, October 8, 2026

Published October 8, 2026 · Cariboo Signals

What the pipeline did, what broke, and what it chose when something broke.

Three runs, two failures, one script

The primary pipeline run failed at 1:05 AM Pacific. The fallback run failed at 2:05 AM Pacific. The second fallback completed this review at 3:05 AM Pacific. The episode shipped.

Both earlier failures occurred during script generation after 120 seconds. The logs are in the GitHub actions. No detail on what broke appears in these facts.

Cohere embeddings did not load

Three pipeline stages tried to call Cohere and hit ModuleNotFoundError. The system fell back to simpler logic in each case:

  • Evolving-story detection: reverted to the non-Cohere path
  • Same-story clustering: reverted to the non-Cohere path
  • Deep-dive ranking: kept the keyword ranking instead of reranking

These are degradations, not failures. The episode still aired. But the deep-dive selection skipped one layer of judgment. The three stories that made it — Grassy Narrows land use, a nuclear data center proposal, and coal miners' black lung benefits — were ranked by keyword match, not by Cohere's semantic model.

Script shipped short, then expanded

The first draft came in at 2072 words against a target of 3400. The system sent it back for one expand pass. What shipped is in quality.script_words, which these facts do not name. The listener heard the expanded version.

Roundup pool larger than one segment could hold

52 stories were cut from the roundup before airtime. This is by design — the pool feeds a budget constraint that caps one segment's count at NEWS_ROUNDUP_COUNT. A cut story can surface again on a day where it matches better.

Three deep dives selected

IndigiNews on land practice near Grassy Narrows. Resilience.org on a 9,000-acre nuclear data center. Mother Jones on black lung benefits for coal miners. All three aired.
