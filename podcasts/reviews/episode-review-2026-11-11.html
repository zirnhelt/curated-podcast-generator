The plan

Roundup frame: A local day of reopenings and repairs, where the wider tech news keeps asking who really controls the things we own and keep running.

Named bridges:

  • The ESP32 board: the parcel camera runs on one, and the next project uses one to read a motorcycle's data bus.
  • Repairability: from an open tool that beats a dealer lock to a teardown that grades a manufacturer's repair commitment.
  • Second-hand hardware: from an old laptop given new work to used memory chips going into new phones.

Handoff to the deep dive: Whether you can keep a device working once its maker stops helping, and where the line falls between fixing it and giving up on it.

Deep-dive question: When a phone breaks, where does repairing it stop making financial sense for a household, and who ends up setting that line, the owner doing the math or the manufacturer pricing the parts?

Riley starts: Riley holds that the repair-versus-replace math is a practical tool households can use, and that repairing is often the smarter spend when phones last longer.
Casey starts: Casey holds that the line is set less by the owner's math than by manufacturer parts pricing, so the sums can quietly steer households toward replacing.

  1. There is a real threshold: repairing makes sense up to a point, and past it buying a new handset is the wiser spend.
    then, because: If the parts price fixes the line, the next question is whether keeping a phone longer still pays when part prices rise.
  2. Longer ownership is a sensible default because phone innovation has plateaued, so an older handset gives up little.
    then, because: Both pressures meet in the total cost of owning a handset, which is what the insurance comparison measures.
  3. Honest repair math has to compare the long-run cost of ownership with and without insurance, not a single repair quote.
    then, because: If the math is only as good as its inputs, a household far from a shop faces inputs the article does not price, which brings it home.
  4. For a Cariboo household weighing a broken phone, the repair-versus-replace math decides whether a repair justifies its cost, and that matters more for families far from a repair shop.
    then, because: That gap leaves the landing: who should publish a price line a household can check, so the threshold is not only the manufacturer's.

Landing: Held open: if parts pricing sets the line between repair and replacement, who should be able to publish and check that line for a household, the manufacturer, an independent tester, or the owner doing their own sums, and what is lost if each assumes another is checking.
