The plan

Roundup frame: A day of fire, flood and fragile landscapes, with a few close-to-home items in between and a hard story from the Chilcotin coast waiting just beyond the headlines.

Named bridges:

  • Wildfire. The next story asks whether AI can spot fires early, and what happens once a fire is spotted.
  • AI tools in wild spaces: from fire sensors to chatbots giving advice to people heading into the backcountry.
  • Fighting wildfire: from Europe's fire season to a California county putting autonomous drones on the fireline.
  • The same El Niño: its first flooding is now hitting the United States.
  • Climate impacts and who is left exposed when they hit.
  • Watching landscapes from a distance: from repeat surveys on the ground to planes sampling the air over the Amazon.

Handoff to the deep dive: Detecting a fire, spotting a hazard or mapping a landscape is one thing, and getting someone on the ground to act on it is another, which is where a grim story from the Bella Coola valley picks up.

Deep-dive question: After a suspected grizzly attack that left two hikers dead near Bella Coola, with officers saying it is not clear how many bears were involved, how should the response weigh public safety, respect for the victims and their families, and the chance of acting against the wrong animal?

Riley starts: Riley holds that a clear, officer-led response that secures the area and removes any dangerous bear is workable, and that the work is to make it as accurate and transparent as possible.
Casey starts: Casey holds that an investigation this uncertain should not be turned into a lesson or a bear removal until it is shown which animal or animals were responsible, and that what is known so far is thin.

  1. The first response was built around people, not bears: secure the area, protect searchers, then recover the dead and bring them home.
    then, because: Once recovery is done, the open question is what officers do about the bear, so the next beat turns to what they know about which animal it was.
  2. The attack is still only suspected, and the officers' own account leaves real uncertainty about the animal or animals involved.
    then, because: If the agency itself is uncertain, the next question is what a backcountry user can fairly take from the story so far.
  3. Bear-safety advice for backcountry users fits this story only loosely, because the public record contains almost nothing yet about how the encounter happened.
    then, because: If advice must wait for facts, the question becomes who in the valley hears what the investigation finds, which brings it to Bella Coola itself.

Landing: The disagreement stays open: when officers cannot yet say how many bears were involved, who decides what counts as enough evidence to act on a particular animal, and what does the valley, as the people who will live with the answer, need to be told along the way.
