# Cross-Episode Synthesis

### Recurring patterns (good)
- **Sustained disagreement without false resolution** is now a consistent strength across the whole week. Nearly every Deep Dive ends with Riley and Casey landing in different places rather than converging — "Agreed on direction. Where we land differently is how much a working tool, on its own, changes who has to say yes" (9/24) and "I'm just not calling it the finish line" (9/20) are model examples. This is the single biggest quality improvement visible in this batch relative to what the dimension rubric is designed to catch.
- **The recurring anchor question** ("Does a place still exist if nobody records it?") is being used skillfully as connective tissue across very different themes (elections, wilderness, off-grid tech, Indigenous fisheries, arts venues) without becoming a gimmick — each episode finds a genuinely different angle on it.
- **Concrete, low-cost, falsifiable proposals** close almost every Deep Dive now (the binder by the breaker box, the standing recorder job description, the three-year DFO citation test, the liaison committee). This is exactly what separates this show from generic tech commentary and should be protected as a house style.

### Recurring patterns (bad)
1. **Land acknowledgement phrasing is repetitive.** Four of six dated episodes use near-identical wording ("on lands that have been cared for by the Secwépemc, Tŝilhqot'in, and Dakelh peoples since time immemorial" — 9/26, 9/25, 9/22; close variants 9/24). Only 9/23 and 9/20 vary meaningfully. Dimension 7 explicitly calls for varied phrasing and this is not happening.
2. **Soft-sourced claims about Indigenous organizations/practices recur as a specific failure mode.** "Williams Lake First Nation, and other Nations in the region, have reportedly worked on layered monitoring for fisheries" (9/25) and "Local First Nations governments have already worked through versions of this on shared community infrastructure" (9/23) are both vague, unnamed, uncited gestures that use Indigenous governance as rhetorical ballast rather than grounded fact. This is precisely the "forced not natural" failure mode Dimension 1 warns against, even in episodes that otherwise handle Indigenous content well.
3. **Named-individual paraphrase risk is showing up 2–3 times a week** at a "plausible but unverifiable" level (Cobb's reported annoyance 9/26, Doerkson's political weight 9/22). None are fabricated outright, but the pattern of hedged-but-unanchored paraphrase around real named people is frequent enough to need a standing check.
4. **Humor is thin on the more "policy-heavy" theme days** (Tuesday Working Lands, Thursday Indigenous Lands, Sunday Science) versus strong on Monday Arts and Saturday Local Affairs. This may be a structural consequence of subject matter, but the mission bar (3+ light moments) is being missed on roughly half the week.
5. **Rural Cariboo Lens is front-loaded-then-thin on exactly the episodes tackling the broadest/most global subject matter** — 9/24 (coastal Indigenous case study) and 9/20 (global science) both pass the letter of Dimension 10's test only in the final stretch, not throughout. The rubric's instruction is being satisfied technically but not in spirit on these two.

- **Too early to judge**: the 2ac2541 "close roadmap findings when the signal stops" change — nothing in these scripts obviously demonstrates a closed-out thread, though the 9/24 episode's "we'd like to track this one properly instead of dropping it today" gestures at ongoing tracking without clear evidence of the underlying mechanism.

### Three most urgent issues
1. **Unanchored claims about named Indigenous organizations/practices and named real politicians** — a recurring, low-grade Dimension 11 problem (9/25, 9/23, 9/26, 9/22) that could become a credibility/consent issue if any of these claims turn out to be unsupported by the actual source articles.
2. **Land acknowledgement repetition** — a simple, easily fixed rotation problem that's been flagged in the rubric and is still happening four episodes running.
3. **Cariboo-specificity arriving too late and too thin in the two most "global" theme days** (Thursday Indigenous, Sunday Science) — risks these becoming the episodes most easily mistaken for generic tech/science content.

## Improvement Action Plan

1. **Problem:** Named real individuals (Cobb, Doerkson) and named real organizations (WLFN, "other Nations in the region") get paraphrased positions or reported claims without a verifiable citation trail — showed up in 09-26, 09-22, 09-25, 09-23.
   **Lever:** `config/prompts.json` → `script_generation_system`, add a new hard constraint block (parallel to the existing HARD CONSTRAINT in `cold_open_generation`).
   **Change:** Add: "HARD CONSTRAINT — NAMED ATTRIBUTION: any sentence attributing a stated position, quote, or documented action to a named real individual or named real organization must be traceable to specific language in the source articles. Hedge words ('reportedly,' 'apparently') do not satisfy this requirement — if the claim isn't in a citation, either cut it or rewrite it as the show's own inference, clearly marked as such (e.g., 'It's a reasonable guess that...' rather than 'X reportedly said...')."
