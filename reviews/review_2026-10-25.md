## Cross-Episode Synthesis

**Recurring good**
- Counterpoints with new information: 10-20 (harvest gap), 10-22 (decision rights, monitoring log) and 10-24 (restraining order vs. ruling) are the model.
- Casey's ledger voice ("Completed is a building. Under construction is a crane on site") and Riley's mechanism-first optimism are distinct when they're not repeating.
- Evidence hedging is generally strong (10-18 Mosqitter, 10-19 India claim types, 10-20 FSC column).

**Recurring bad**
1. **A single rhetorical engine across all episodes.** Nearly every Deep Dive ends in some version of "who keeps the record/log/binder in year five" and "put it in the contract with a name attached." Fine as a Casey lens, but both hosts now voice it (Riley: "A named role and a reporting date answer both. Put them in the contract"). Combined with the weekly anchor, episodes converge on the same four moves regardless of theme. The 10-21 closing (binder, on-call name, log, again) is the extreme case.
2. **Signposted structure.** "Now the X," "Take X," "Same test for Y," "Then look at Z," "Start in X" are the soft form of the banned pre-announcement. They appear in every episode and carry the Deep Dives as catalogues rather than arguments.
3. **Catalogue Deep Dives.** 10-18 (six topics), 10-21 (hub, solar, Nebraska, Musk, Congo) and 10-24 (candidate roll-call) lose the "one question, pursued" shape. The producer flagged 10-18 as incoherent and 10-20 as loosely connected.
4. **Humor is nearly absent.** Most episodes land 1–2 light moments against a target of 3. 10-24 has none.
5. **Missing First Nations decision-makers in non-Thursday episodes.** Only 10-22 names a Cariboo Nation or Indigenous organization in the Deep Dive. The Mission says Indigenous context should be natural throughout.
6. **Repetition within an episode.** 10-21 repeats the microwave-solar content (confirmed by the producer) and the binder idea three or four times.
7. **Closing-line drift.** 10-22's post-sign-off confession about not speaking to First Nations communications staff is stranded after the goodbye.
8. **Out-of-theme or out-of-region material.** The Hormuz segment (10-18), the Alberta referendum and Musk (10-21), the Columbia River-Revelstoke riding (10-23) and the Ring of Fire road (10-22) are all stretched to fit.

**Did the prompt/code changes show up?**
- **Voice cards (10-18 onward):** partially. Casey's "honest ledger" and "maintenance is the real test" cards are visible, arguably over-visible: the year-five line is now a tic. Riley's "turn the one number into an everyday equivalent" card is largely missing (no mechanism-in-one-sentence move or everyday-equivalent translation stands out, apart from "Forty percent of 700 is roughly 280 buildings in five years"). Riley's "tinkerer's curiosity" shows up in 10-21. The "wry self-correction aside" card barely appears, which probably explains the low humor scores. No host voiced a personal anecdote or family reference, so that rule held.
- **Kagi News cluster bodies and `_kite_context` (10-22, 10-23):** can't judge from these scripts since the roundups are omitted, but the producer's 10-21 "just a title" note predates the Kagi fix, and the 10-24 notes suggest the roundup clustering and cross-episode memory are still weak. Too early to judge.
- **Roundup-order and voice-ratio roadmap changes:** the producer's 10-24 note ("weird topic jumping at the end of the roundup") suggests the story-placement fix has not fully landed.

**Theme adherence across the week:** The rotation does real work on Tuesday (10-20), Thursday (10-22) and Saturday (10-24). It is thin on Sunday (10-18), where Hormuz and Mosqitter dilute the science theme, and on Wednesday (10-21), where Musk and Alberta drift to politics. On Monday and Friday the theme is present but front-loaded, then abandoned for civic or political tangents. The episodes converge on one shape: a trust question, a catalogue of stories, then a "who keeps the record" close.

**Most urgent issues**
1. The shared year-five/binder/log closing engine, plus signposted catalogue structure.
2. Deep Dive coherence and repetition (10-18, 10-21), plus out-of-theme stretches.
3. Candidate and election-coverage hygiene (10-24) and unverified named quotes (10-20 Miller, 10-23 Kubala).
